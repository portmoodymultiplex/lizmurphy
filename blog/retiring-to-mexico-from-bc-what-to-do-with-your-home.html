Retiring to Mexico from BC: sell, rent, or keep the house?

For most people, the BC home is the biggest asset they own. What you do with it shapes everything else about the move.

The Second Coast

By Liz Murphy, REALTOR® · Zada Group · 6 min read

Most of the Mexico conversations I have don't start with a condo in Bucerías. They start with a townhouse in the suburbs, a house the kids grew up in, or a condo that's been paid off for years. The question underneath is almost always the same: what do we do with this place?

I've been on both ends of it. In 2004 I moved my family to Mexico and lived there for seven years. Today I sell homes in Greater Vancouver. So here's how I'd think about it.

1. Snowbird or full move?

Before you look at a single listing, get clear on what kind of Mexico life you actually want. There are two common paths.

  • The snowbird. You keep your BC home and spend the winter months in Mexico. Lower commitment, but two homes to look after.
  • The full move. You sell in BC and make Mexico home base. More freedom and usually more money in your pocket, but a bigger leap.

A few honest questions help sort it out. How many months a year do you actually want to be there? Do you need to stay close to kids, grandkids or aging parents here? Would you rather have your equity working for you, or keep a home to come back to? And have you spent a few weeks in Bucerías in both high season and low season? June is a different town than January.

2. What the equity can look like

Here in Metro Vancouver, in a city like New Westminster, a typical two-bedroom, two-bathroom condo sells for roughly $600,000 to $700,000, and a townhome for roughly $1 million to $1.2 million.

An illustration, not a real client: a couple owns a mortgage-free townhome worth about $1.1 million. They sell and buy a two-bedroom condo in Bucerías for around US$350,000, which is about C$480,000 to C$500,000 depending on the exchange rate. After costs on both ends, that can leave well over half a million dollars for retirement, travel, and the occasional margarita.

Same couple, different choice: they keep the townhome and snowbird. That means a second property to carry and a different set of rules to follow. Neither answer is wrong. They're just very different lives.

3. Three options for the BC home

Sell and move

Best for a full-time move. It frees up your equity, there are no landlord headaches, and it's simpler if you sell before you leave. The catch: it's hard to re-enter the BC market later, and selling after you've become a non-resident of Canada gets complicated.

Keep it and rent it out

Best for keeping a way back, plus income. You keep your foot in the BC market. But your home becomes a rental for tax purposes, rent is taxed differently once you're a non-resident, and you're managing tenants from 3,000 kilometres away. Most people who choose this hire a property manager.

Keep it and snowbird

Best for splitting the year. You keep a home base in BC and family close by. Watch for BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax if the home sits empty, insurance rules for vacant homes, and MSP residency rules.

4. MSP and how long you can be away

This one catches people. According to the Province, BC residents who are outside the province for vacation can be away a total of up to seven months in a calendar year and keep their MSP coverage. There's also a one-time extended absence of up to 24 months, available once every five years, with conditions attached.

If you're away more than that, you can lose coverage. Puerto Vallarta, about 30 minutes from Bucerías, has well-regarded private hospitals, and many expats carry private health insurance. Either way, call Health Insurance BC before you plan a long stay, and build the trip around the real number.

5. Questions to ask your accountant

I'm not an accountant, and this is the part where you need one, ideally someone who does cross-border work. Bring these:

  • If I sell now, is my whole gain covered by the principal residence exemption?
  • What happens, tax-wise, if I become a non-resident of Canada?
  • If I sell after I've moved, what is a clearance certificate and how long will it hold up my money?
  • If I turn my home into a rental, what are the tax consequences, and are there elections I should make?
  • How is rent taxed if I'm living in Mexico?
  • Will the Speculation and Vacancy Tax apply to my situation?
A tip from experience: selling before you leave is almost always simpler than selling from Mexico.

6. Sell first or buy first?

  • Sell first. You know exactly what you have to spend, and you're not carrying two properties. You may need a short-term rental in between.
  • Buy first. You lock in the right place in Bucerías, but you may carry two properties for a while.
  • The middle path. Sell in BC, rent in Bucerías for a season, then buy once you know which street you love.

Whichever you pick, it helps when the person selling your BC home and the people helping you buy in Mexico are actually talking to each other. That's how I work: I handle the sale here, and I connect you with Bienvenidos Real Estate in Bucerías, the brokerage my family started in 2010.

7. Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to live in Mexico full time?

For temporary residency in 2026, Mexico asks you to show roughly US$4,300 to $4,500 a month in income over the last six months, or roughly US$73,000 to $75,000 in average savings over the last year. The thresholds change every year and vary by consulate, so check with the Mexican consulate in Vancouver before you apply.

Can I sell my BC home after I've already moved to Mexico?

Yes, but it's more complicated once you're a non-resident, and your proceeds can be held up while the paperwork clears. Ask your accountant about clearance certificates before you decide on timing.

Can I rent out my Mexico condo while I'm back in BC?

Many owners do. A two-bed, two-bath condo in Bucerías rents for roughly US$2,000 to $3,000 a month plus utilities, and property management typically runs about 20% of rental income. That income is taxable in Mexico and may need to be reported in Canada too.
